## What failed, in plain language

An engineer adds a logic analyser to a design so they can watch signals inside the chip
while it runs. The chip programs correctly. The tools confirm the analyser is present in
the built design. But when the engineer connects, the analyser is not there.

The obvious suspect is the clock, because a circuit with no clock cannot answer. Every
clock setting checks out. It is not the clock.

The real cause is a wall. This chip has a processor half and a programmable half, and the
two are separated by a power barrier that is held shut until firmware explicitly asks for
it to be opened. The startup code contains the routine that opens it and never calls it.
So the clock is switched on at the source and no ticks ever arrive on the other side.

## The wrong hypothesis

The clock. Specifically `C_ENABLE_CLK_DIVIDER` and every `BSCAN_SWITCH_USER_MASK` value,
none of which made any difference, because all of them are irrelevant when the debug hub
has no clock reaching it at all.

This is the part of the case worth reading. The tool's own error text points at the debug
hub, and the resolution notes point at clocking, so the documented path leads directly
into the hypothesis that cannot work.

## Root cause

`pl_clk0` is the only clock net in the block design. It is generated by the PS and must
cross the PS-PL boundary through voltage level shifters whose enables are gated by the PL
power domain (UG1085 p.34). That wall comes down by a PMU service request, not by clock
configuration.

`psu_ps_pl_isolation_removal_data()` exists in the XSA's own `psu_init.c`, but `psu_init()`
never calls it. Its only callers sit inside FSBL, and every one of their guards fails in a
JTAG side-loaded flow.

So `CRL_APB` reports the clock as active while no edges reach the fabric.

## Before and after

Measured on `CSU_PCAP_STATUS` (`0xFFCA3010`):

    before  0x80000FD2    PL_INIT=0  PL_DONE=0  pcfg_gwe=0  pl_cfg_used=0
    after   0xA0002FDE    PL_INIT=1  PL_DONE=1  pcfg_gwe=1  pl_cfg_used=1

Vivado then returns `hw_ila_1` at depth 2048.

## The fix and what it cost

Two register writes plus a status read-back. Bit 23 is PL in all three PMU_GLOBAL
registers.

Cost: no area, no timing, no power impact on the design. The cost is a bring-up ordering
constraint, which is the kind of cost that does not show up in any report and is the
reason this case is worth writing down.

The full sequence, the corrected `PCAP_STATUS` bit map, two registers that actively
mislead during debug, and the working bring-up order are in the source document.

## How to reproduce

You need a ZCU104 and Vivado. Follow the working order in the source document, and report
what you saw in an issue whether it matched or not.

This case cannot be reproduced without that hardware, which is a real limitation. Cases
that reproduce on free tools with no board are a better entry point for most contributors.
